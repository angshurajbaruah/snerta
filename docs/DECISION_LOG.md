## 2026-11-13 — Milestone 2b: calibration rebuilt after the first real-phone run

| # | Decision | Why | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| D27 | **The tray is now the whole screen**, inset only by the safe areas and a 64 px control strip at the bottom. It was a bento card in the middle of the page. | The first phone run sampled only **34%–69% of the screen height**. Every target sat in the comfortable band, so of course they were all easy — the founder reached all nine without effort and said so. The part one-handed reach actually fails at, the top of the screen where the wordmark sits, was never measured. The flat latency spread (676 ms top vs 533 ms bottom) was an artefact of testing nine easy positions, not a weak effect. | Yes |
| D28 | **Grid 3×3 → 3×5**, fractions `[0.06, 0.28, 0.5, 0.72, 0.94]` down and `[0.12, 0.5, 0.88]` across. 15 positions × 2 passes = **30 measured taps**. | Five rows give real vertical resolution across the reach gradient instead of three clustered in the middle. The edge insets are the tightest that keep every 52 px hit square fully on the tray at 320×480; a test pins this, as does one that no two targets come within a hit square of each other. 3×5 also matches what board 03 drew. | Yes — constants |
| D29 | **Three unrecorded practice taps** before the measured sequence, drawn from the middle rows only. | The run's first two samples were noise: four stray taps in the first 3.7 s, then 6918 ms on the second target against a 560 ms median. Practice on easy targets means nobody is still working out the task on real data. Standard for reaction-time work. | Yes |
| D30 | **The layout cannot shift during calibration.** Instructions float over the tray in a card instead of sitting above it; the tray is `position: absolute` against a fixed root, so it is laid out once. | Pressing Start used to remove four lines of instructions, which grew the tray and moved every dot. The founder reported "each dot was moving… so abruptly" and the data showed the cost. Verified: 0 of 15 dots move when Start is pressed. Same rule as D25, which had only been applied to the comparison screen. | No — keep |
| D31 | Done targets no longer change appearance; only the active one does, growing about its own centre via `transform: scale()` so the tap point never moves. | Less visual churn, and the one thing that changes is the thing you are meant to look at. | Yes |
| D32 | During the run all top chrome is hidden; progress and Stop sit in the bottom strip, outside the tray's box. | The wordmark would otherwise overlap the top row of targets once the safe-area inset pushes them down on a notched phone, and a centred Stop button would have swallowed taps meant for the bottom-centre target. The bottom is the easy zone, so giving up a strip there costs little. | Yes |

## 2026-11-13 — Milestone 3: comfort self-report and reach map

| # | Decision | Why | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| D38 | The self-report covers all **15 zones**, laid out in the same positions as the tray, and cycles Not sure → Easy → Stretch → Hard → Not sure on tap. Every zone starts at **Not sure** and staying there is a recorded answer. | The brief's rule: Unknown stays Unknown, and nobody is forced to classify a zone they have no opinion about. Cycling back round means no state is a trap. | Yes |
| D39 | **Nothing maps a latency to a label, anywhere.** The two live in separate functions, separate screens and separate export fields. A test asserts the module exposes no export matching `/infer|derive|classif|labelFrom|fromLatency/`. | Deriving comfort from speed is the single inference the brief rules out. Making it absent by construction beats remembering not to do it. | No — keep |
| D40 | The map shows **what you reported** and **what the clock recorded** as two sections, the second on a dark bento with its limits stated inline. | They are different kinds of claim. Putting the timing inside the map would make it look like evidence of difficulty, which it is not. | No — keep |
| D41 | New **"Where the two disagree"** section: zones called Easy that took ≥1.6× the median, or called Hard that were tapped at ≤0.9×. Neither account overrules the other. | This is the control for the whole instrument. The founder's second run showed top-left at 3.1× the middle; whether that is difficulty or just distance from where the eyes rest is only answerable by the person. Surfacing the disagreement is the finding, not an error to smooth away. | No — keep |
| D42 | `rankZones` marks quickest and slowest **only when the spread is ≥1.5×**, otherwise nothing is marked. | A flat set of numbers should not be dressed up as a pattern. | Yes — threshold |
| D43 | Export schema → **`snerta-0.2`**. Adds `selfReport` (per zone, unknowns preserved), `gripChanged`, and `latencyByZoneMs` as a sibling field, with a limitation line stating neither was derived from the other. | A reader of the file must be able to see that the labels are the person's and the timings are the machine's. | Yes |
| D44 | A skipped calibration goes **straight to the demo**: no comfort question, no map. | There is nothing measured to label, and asking anyway would invite a map built on nothing. | Yes |
| D45 | Easy renders dark, Hard light, following the canvas's Natural/Stretch/Hard legend — but every cell carries its label as text and the legend repeats it. The unlabelled dash was darkened from `--steel` to `--graphite` after the audit measured it at 2.93:1. | Greyscale only, and never colour alone. The audit caught the dash; it is the sole visual cue for an unlabelled zone, so it has to clear AA. | Yes |
